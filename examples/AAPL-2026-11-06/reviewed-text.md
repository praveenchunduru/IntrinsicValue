# Apple Inc. (AAPL) — Investment Committee Equity Research Draft

**Memo Date:** 2026-11-06 | **Quote Observation Date:** 2026-11-05 (regular-session close, 16:00 ET) | **Currency:** USD | **FY2026 Reported Period End:** September 26, 2026 (52 weeks, per FY2026 10-K) [S18]

## Executive Valuation View

The house probability-weighted intrinsic value is approximately **$151.71 per share**, derived from a scenario-weighted FCFF model anchored to fiscal 2026 reported actuals. The regular-session observed price of **$344.17** [S1] implies a premium of approximately 126.9% to that weighted estimate, or a weighted downside of approximately **55.9%** from current levels. 

The draft committee decision under current house assumptions is to avoid initiating at the observed $344.17 price. A committee preferring the lower-WACC, higher-durability contrary case should explicitly replace the house inputs and recompute the result. The valuation conclusion is conditional. The house model produces three scenarios — bear ($88.25), base ($152.31), and bull ($213.99) — none of which reaches the market price. The reverse-DCF solves one specific combination (base-case EBIT margins, sales-to-capital 3.0x, WACC 9.5%, tax 17%, terminal growth 3.0%, terminal ROIC 18%) for a constant annual revenue growth rate of approximately **22.9%** [computed reverse_dcf]. The model found one solution within the search range of −50% to +50% annual growth; uniqueness outside that range is not established. Separately, a model-computed conditional counterfactual (15% growth, 36% EBIT margins, 8.0% WACC, other base inputs fixed) yields **$342.44 per share** [computed market_case_15pct_36pct_8pct], within $1.73 of the market price. Discounted terminal value supplies approximately 77.5% of this case's enterprise value [computed market_case_15pct_36pct_8pct]. These calculations do not cover every possible input combination. No necessary joint conditions for all market-closing combinations have been proved; the house conclusion rests on none of the three selected house scenarios reaching the market price.

The house thesis is that sustainable revenue growth is materially lower than the rate implied by the market price under base assumptions. Management identifies near-term memory-cost and AI ramp pressure [S20]; its persistence and offsets remain uncertain. Regulatory outcomes could pressure parts of Services economics, although the supplied evidence does not isolate App Store exposure precisely. The thesis is weakened — though not necessarily closed — if Apple demonstrates durable double-digit revenue growth, stable or expanding gross margins, and regulatory outcomes that leave App Store economics substantially intact. Those observations would change named growth, margin and risk assumptions and require recomputation; they do not automatically overturn the valuation view. The growth-only reverse DCF and the separate joint counterfactual are conditional calculations with different fixed inputs. Neither establishes a unique set of market expectations or necessary conditions across all possible input combinations.

## Variant Thesis and What the Market Price Implies

One possible market-pricing case rests on three beliefs: that AI integration accelerates services monetization across the installed base, that the services revenue category can sustain high-teens growth despite regulatory pressure, and that Apple's hardware refresh cycle — anchored by the iPhone Duo and the new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display [S3, S4] — extends the premium-device replacement cycle.

The strongest contrary explanation is that investors require a lower return and expect durable growth and margins, rather than the growth-only reverse DCF. The 15%/36%/8% counterfactual makes that alternative model-consistent near the observed price. A sustained failure of growth or margins to approach those assumptions, or evidence supporting a higher required return, would weaken that particular explanation; near-term cost guidance alone does not refute seven-year durability. The house variant is narrower. Fiscal 2026 revenue grew 15.1% year-over-year to $478.8 billion [S7, S18], the highest of the three available year-over-year comparisons in the supplied series (FY2024: 2.0%, FY2025: 6.4%, FY2026: 15.1%) [S2 derived_metrics]; FY2023 is the base year with no prior-year comparison available. The annual FY2026 gross margin was **48.8%** [S18 E3, S21]; the Q4 FY2026 gross margin of 47.9% included a favorable impact of approximately one percentage point from tariff refunds on margin — not on revenue — and $0.05 on diluted EPS [S3, S21]. Separately, management guided Q1 FY2027 gross margin to 45–46%, citing higher memory costs and Siri AI ramp costs [S20]; that is a forward-looking management disclosure, not an extrapolation from the annual result. R&D expense grew from $34.6 billion in FY2025 to $45.8 billion in FY2026 [S18 E3], an $11.2 billion increase driven by higher infrastructure costs including AI investments and headcount-related expenses per management characterization in the 10-K MD&A (specific sub-component breakdown not available in the supplied excerpts) [S19]; the AI-specific portion is not separately disclosed and the full increment is not a reliable AI-spend proxy. If similar cost growth is not offset by revenue, mix or efficiency gains, it could pressure operating margin relative to FY2026's 33.0% [S18 E3].

One illustrative conditional point: a model-computed conditional counterfactual (15% growth, 36% EBIT margins, 8.0% WACC, other base inputs fixed) yields **$342.44 per share** [computed market_case_15pct_36pct_8pct], within $1.73 of the observed market price. This requires sustained margin expansion well above FY2026 actuals (36% versus the reported 33.0%), a discount rate materially below the house base case (8.0% versus 9.5%), and a terminal-value fraction that is heavily weighted toward post-forecast assumptions (approximately 77.5% of enterprise value). This particular case sustains roughly FY2026's growth rate while increasing margins. Near-term memory-cost pressure [S20] and regulatory proceedings [S11, S12] challenge that path without ruling out offsetting benefits. Other combinations remain untested, and partial input changes move value continuously. The house conclusion rests on none of the three selected scenarios reaching the market price.

## Business Economics and Industry Competition

Apple operates across two revenue categories: hardware products ($355.5 billion in FY2026) and services ($123.3 billion) [S7, S18 E3]. These are not GAAP reportable segments; Apple's GAAP reportable segments are geographic — Americas, Europe, Greater China, Japan, and Rest of Asia Pacific [S5, S7, S19]. The economic analysis below uses the products and services revenue categories for margin and competitive discussion, not GAAP segment reporting.

The hardware revenue category is subject to component cost cycles — most visibly DRAM and NAND pricing — that Apple cannot fully control. The services revenue category carries structurally higher gross margins: services cost of sales was $29.4 billion on $123.3 billion revenue in FY2026, implying approximately 76% gross margin on services [S7, S21], versus 39.3% on products [S21]. The annual FY2026 total gross margin was 48.8% [S21], up from 46.9% in FY2025, with the improvement driven by product mix and tariff refunds partially offset by higher costs including memory [S21].

The competitive position in hardware is characterized by minority global unit share but dominant premium-segment economics [S5]. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that Apple holds a minority share in global smartphone, PC, tablet, and wearables markets, and that competitors have the resources to price at or below cost [S5]. The CMA's October 2025 SMS designation found that Apple has substantial and entrenched market power in its Mobile Platform in the UK, with low switching rates, strong network effects, and high returns on capital [S11]. That finding is simultaneously evidence of durable competitive position and a gateway to conduct requirements that could alter the economics. The 22% terminal ROIC in the house bull case is a house assumption reflecting a judgment about sustained competitive moat; the CMA finding provides qualitative context but does not quantify or endorse any specific ROIC rate.

An unverified secondary excerpt describes legal and regulatory restrictions on Apple's developer fees [S10]. A government-document search excerpt describes the UK platform designation [S11]; a secondary news excerpt describes a DOJ lawsuit alleging monopolistic practices across digital wallets, app distribution, messaging, and cloud gaming [S12]. These reported proceedings do not quantify App Store revenue exposure or realized fee impact. The DOJ allegations concern broader platform conduct; a specific App Store fee-pressure pathway and its earnings effect remain analyst risk inferences. The 10-K's competition section notes that AI technologies are intensifying competitive dynamics [S5]. The new Siri AI and Apple Intelligence features [S3, S4] represent Apple's response, but the evidence does not establish that these features generate incremental revenue rather than defensive retention.

Capital intensity is low relative to revenue. FY2026 capital expenditure was $9.0 billion on $478.8 billion revenue [S18 E5], approximately 1.9%. The house model uses a sales-to-capital ratio of 3.0x in the base case. That assumption is consistent with the asset-light manufacturing model but does not account for the possibility that AI infrastructure investment — which contributed to the R&D surge — may require higher capital intensity going forward.

## Management and Capital Allocation

John Ternus became CEO and Timothy D. Cook transitioned to Executive Chair of the Board, as confirmed by the FY2026 10-K signatures [S18 E10]. Kevan Parekh serves as CFO [S18 E10]. The evidence does not permit an assessment of whether Ternus's strategic priorities differ materially from Cook's, but the 10-K and earnings release reflect continuity in capital return policy and product strategy.

Capital allocation in FY2026 was heavily weighted toward shareholder returns. The cash-flow statement reports $86.390 billion in share repurchases and $15.721 billion in dividends [S18 E5], for combined cash returns of approximately **$102.1 billion**, representing approximately **70.4%** of the $145.097 billion operating cash flow — where the 70.4% calculation uses the cash-flow-statement repurchase figure of $86.390 billion [S18 E5]. Separately, the capital return program disclosure [S18 E7] and Note 11 [S19 E9] both state $85.8 billion for the same period; the $0.59 billion difference between the cash-flow-statement figure ($86.390 billion) and the Note 11 figure ($85.8 billion) is unreconciled in the available evidence, and no cause for the difference is asserted. In April 2026, the board authorized a new $100 billion repurchase program and raised the quarterly dividend from $0.26 to $0.27 per share [S18 E7].

The diluted weighted-average share count declined from 15.81 billion in FY2023 to 14.72 billion in FY2026 [S2, S18 E3]; these are annual weighted-average diluted shares, not period-end counts. Separately, period-end shares outstanding fell by approximately 241 million in FY2026 — 296.7 million shares repurchased less 55.7 million shares issued net of employee tax withholding per Note 11 [S19 E9]. These are different measures with different dates; declining average shares is consistent with buyback activity but does not by itself prove the pace or economics of repurchases. Stock-based compensation was $13.8 billion in FY2026 [S18 E5], retained in GAAP EBIT; no separate model deduction is made. The repurchase program is discretionary and non-obligatory [S18 E7]; if shares are repurchased below intrinsic value, value per remaining share increases all else equal, but execution at prices above intrinsic value is value-destructive relative to the house estimate.

The remaining operating cash flow after capital returns was absorbed by, among other uses: capital expenditure ($9.0 billion), net marketable securities purchases ($5.85 billion, derived from S19 E5 as purchases of $54,945 million less maturities of $34,815 million less sales of $14,280 million), term debt repayments ($12.4 billion), net commercial paper reduction ($5.9 billion), equity-award tax withholding ($6.8 billion), other investing and financing items ($2.6 billion combined), and a net cash increase of $0.4 billion [S19 E5]. The full cash-flow identity is: $145.097 billion OCF = $102.111 billion cash returns + $9.041 billion capex + $5.850 billion net securities purchases + $12.396 billion term debt repayments + $5.911 billion net CP repayment + $6.793 billion equity-award tax withholding + $2.389 billion other investing + $0.235 billion other financing + $0.371 billion cash increase [S19 E5].

The governance structure — with Cook as Executive Chair — introduces a question about board independence that the available evidence does not resolve. No proxy statement or governance disclosure is included in the evidence set.

## Historical Financial Quality

The four-year series uses secondary normalized annual data [S2], with the latest three years checked against the supplied FY2026 filing [S18]. S2's September 30 labels are normalized dates, not exact fiscal-period ends; its FY2023 values are not comprehensively reconciled to an archived FY2023 filing. On that basis, the series shows the following pattern. Revenue grew 2.0% in FY2024, 6.4% in FY2025, and 15.1% in FY2026 [S2 derived_metrics]; FY2023 is the base year with no prior-year comparison in the supplied series. Net margin was 25.3% in FY2023, 24.0% in FY2024, 26.9% in FY2025, and 27.6% in FY2026 [S2 derived_metrics]. Operating cash flow exceeded net income in three of four years; the exception was FY2025, where OCF of $111.5 billion was $528 million below net income of $112.0 billion [S2 derived_metrics]. Changes in operating assets and liabilities reduced OCF by approximately $25.0 billion in FY2025 and $14.9 billion in FY2026 [S19 E5]. These were cash outflows in both years; the smaller FY2026 drag, together with noncash adjustments, contributed to improved cash conversion. The FY2026 OCF of $145.1 billion exceeded net income by $12.9 billion [S2 derived_metrics], driven by D&A of $13.4 billion and SBC of $13.8 billion partially offset by working capital movements [S18 E5].

The effective tax rate was volatile: 14.7% in FY2023 [S2, S36], 24.1% in FY2024, 15.6% in FY2025, and 17.0% in FY2026 [S2, S18 E3, S21]. The FY2023 rate of 14.7% is computed from secondary normalized data ($16,741 million tax provision divided by $113,736 million pretax income [S2]); an unverified search excerpt from an SEC-hosted tax reconciliation also reports 14.7% [S36]. That excerpt is corroborating evidence, not a full primary-document reconciliation. The FY2024 spike to 24.1% reflects a disclosed one-time Ireland tax charge per Note 8 of the 10-K [S19, S37]; this is a one-time item, not a structural rate shift. The FY2026 rate of 17.0% was higher than FY2025's 15.6% primarily due to a higher effective tax rate on foreign earnings, the impact of foreign currency loss regulations, and foreign currency revaluations in Q1 FY2025 related to the State Aid Decision [S21]; it is not solely attributable to any single item. The house model applies a uniform 17% cash operating tax rate across all scenarios, consistent with the FY2026 reported rate and treated as the normalized forward rate. The computed_diagnostics show that raising the uniform tax rate to 20% reduces the weighted per-share value by approximately $5.50 (3.6%), and raising it to 24% reduces it by approximately $12.82 (8.5%), with all other scenario inputs fixed [computed_diagnostics]. These are material but not thesis-changing at the current market price premium.

Total debt declined from $111.1 billion in FY2023 to $80.1 billion in FY2026 [S2]. Cash, equivalents, and marketable securities totaled $136.9 billion as of September 26, 2026 [S18 E6], producing house-modeled net cash of approximately $56.8 billion under the policy that treats all cash and marketable securities as nonoperating assets [computed net_debt_bridge]. The house bridge classifies all reported cash and cash equivalents ($36.3 billion) plus current ($20.4 billion) and noncurrent ($80.2 billion) marketable securities as nonoperating assets; this is an explicit house modeling policy and does not establish that all cash is economically surplus or that required operating cash is zero [S19 E4]. The company's liquidity disclosure states that cash, securities, and ongoing operations are sufficient for requirements over the next 12 months and beyond [S18 E6]; this is an issuer statement and should be read with that attribution.

Disclosed contractual obligations as of September 26, 2026 include: manufacturing purchase obligations ($89.7 billion total, $82.7 billion within 12 months), other purchase obligations ($27.3 billion total, $9.9 billion within 12 months), lease obligations ($17.8 billion total, $2.8 billion within 12 months), term debt principal ($78.7 billion total, $10.0 billion within 12 months), future note interest ($34.4 billion total, $2.4 billion within 12 months), and commercial paper ($2.0 billion within 12 months) [S18 E6]. Manufacturing purchase obligations represent future operating commitments, not an additional deduction from historical FY2026 OCF. Their future settlement must be assessed within projected operating cash flows; the gross amount should not be deducted again without checking what the forecast already includes.

## Operating Forecast and Valuation

The house model is an unlevered FCFF framework using incremental sales-to-capital reinvestment, with three scenarios probability-weighted at 25% bear, 50% base, and 25% bull. All forecasts and scenario weights are house assumptions, not reported facts or company guidance. All forecast EBIT margins use the GAAP operating-income convention, retaining operating rent, SBC and R&D. Under that convention, the equity bridge does not deduct an operating lease liability separately.

**Base case** (50% weight): Revenue grows at 7–8% annually through the mid-forecast period, decelerating to 4% by year seven, reflecting normalized hardware cycles and mid-single-digit services growth. EBIT margins hold near 33%, consistent with FY2026 actuals, with modest fade as AI operating costs scale. The sales-to-capital ratio is 3.0x. WACC is **9.5%**, which is a separately selected house discount-rate judgment, not derived from any third-party model inputs. Terminal growth is 3.0%, terminal ROIC 18%.

A material modeling disclosure: terminal reinvestment in the base case is governed by the terminal ROIC of 18%, not by continuing the forecast sales-to-capital of 3.0x. This produces a step-down in FCFF at the terminal boundary — terminal FCFF of $169.8 billion versus year-7 forecast FCFF of $188.3 billion, a decline of approximately **9.8%** [computed_diagnostics base terminal_transition]. This is a deliberate competitive-fade assumption reflecting the expectation that incremental returns moderate as the business matures. If instead the forecast sales-to-capital of 3.0x were continued into the terminal period, the base-case per-share value would be approximately $167.20 rather than $152.31 [computed_diagnostics]. The same terminal-ROIC governance applies in the bear and bull cases: the bear case produces a terminal FCFF step-down of approximately **13.0%** (year-7 FCFF $121.8 billion to terminal FCFF $105.9 billion), and the bull case produces a step-down of approximately **7.1%** (year-7 FCFF $234.1 billion to terminal FCFF $217.5 billion) [computed_diagnostics terminal_transition].

The base-case per-share value is approximately **$152.31** [computed].

**Bear case** (25% weight): The house assumes regulatory intervention leads to material App Store fee reductions, memory cost inflation extends beyond the December 2026 quarter, and R&D and AI infrastructure spending grows without proportionate revenue. S20 establishes only near-term cost guidance; the longer duration and economic outcomes are scenario assumptions. Revenue growth fades to 2–4% annually. EBIT margins enter at approximately 30.0% in year 1 — approximately 300 basis points below FY2026's 33.0% — and compress further to approximately 26.5% by year 7, a total compression of approximately 651 basis points from the FY2026 base [computed bear cashflows]. WACC rises to 10.5% to reflect elevated regulatory and execution risk. Terminal growth is 2.5%, terminal ROIC 14%. Terminal FCFF steps down approximately 13.0% at the terminal boundary [computed_diagnostics]. If instead the forecast sales-to-capital of 2.8x were continued into the terminal period rather than governed by terminal ROIC (14%), the bear-case value would be approximately **$95.91 per share** [computed_diagnostics]. Bear-case per-share value under the house terminal-ROIC assumption is approximately **$88.25** [computed].

**Bull case** (25% weight): AI-powered Siri and Apple Intelligence drive accelerated services monetization across the installed base [S3, S4, S5]. The installed base of active devices reached a new all-time high across all major product categories and geographic segments [S3]; further expansion supports services revenue. Regulatory outcomes are manageable. The house bull case assumes gross margin rises above 49% from FY2026's 48.8% [S18 E3] as memory costs normalize and services mix rises. S20 supports near-term cost pressure and Q1 guidance, not this assumed recovery; the Q4 FY2026 gross margin of 47.9% included approximately one percentage point of tariff-refund benefit on margin, not on revenue [S3, S21]. iPhone Duo cycle sustains hardware momentum [S3]. Revenue grows at 10–11% near-term, below FY2026's 15.1%, fading to 6% by year 7. WACC is 9.0%, terminal growth 3.5%, terminal ROIC 22% (house assumption). The $100 billion repurchase authorization [S18 E7] is discretionary and non-obligatory; if executed below intrinsic value, value per remaining share increases all else equal, but no minimum acquisition is guaranteed. Terminal FCFF steps down approximately 7.1% at the terminal boundary; if forecast sales-to-capital of 3.2x were continued instead, the bull-case value would be approximately $235.48 per share [computed_diagnostics]. Bull-case per-share value under the house terminal-ROIC assumption is approximately **$213.99** [computed].

The probability-weighted value is approximately **$151.71** [computed]. The market price of $344.17 [S1] implies a premium of approximately 126.9% to this estimate.

## Bear, Base, and Bull Scenarios

**Bear:** App Store fee pressure, prolonged component-cost inflation and weak returns on AI spending reduce revenue growth and operating margins. Strong FY2026 services and Greater China results challenge the assumed abrupt slowdown, although they do not preclude future deterioration [S7]. The model's $88.25 value reflects the explicit bear forecasts above.

**Base:** Hardware replacement cycles normalize while services expand. Growth fades from 7–8% to 4%; EBIT margins drift from approximately 33% to 32.2%. The $152.31 value also reflects an immediate reduction in assumed capital efficiency at the terminal boundary, rather than a gradual observed fade [computed].

**Bull:** AI features improve services monetization, hardware demand remains strong, and regulatory outcomes are manageable. The model assumes 10–11% near-term revenue growth fading to 6%, with EBIT margins of 33.5–34.5%. Its $213.99 value remains below the observed price. The separately computed $342.44 counterfactual uses different growth, margin and discount-rate inputs; it has no house probability and does not enter the weighted estimate.

## Limits of the Conditional Market-Price Case

The reverse-DCF solution of 22.9% constant annual revenue growth (under fixed base-case inputs) is a conditional solution under those specific assumptions, not a description of market beliefs or the house bull scenario [computed reverse_dcf]. The model found one solution within the search range of −50% to +50% annual growth; uniqueness outside that range is not established. The house bull assumes 10–11% near-term growth fading to 6% by year 7 — materially lower than 22.9%.

At 22.9% growth on a $478.8 billion base, year-1 incremental revenue is approximately **$109.8 billion per the reverse-DCF model** [computed reverse_dcf revenue_path year 1], approximately 89% of FY2026 services revenue ($123.3 billion [S7]). No evidence in the filings supports that trajectory on a sustained basis. The FY2026 revenue growth of 15.1% is the highest of the three available year-over-year comparisons in the supplied series [S2 derived_metrics]; Q4 FY2026 gross margin of 47.9% included approximately one percentage point of tariff-refund benefit on margin (not on revenue) [S3, S21], and the underlying annual gross margin of 48.8% faces near-term pressure from memory costs [S20].

The model-computed conditional counterfactual assumes constant 15% revenue growth and 36% EBIT margins over seven years, with an 8.0% WACC. All other base inputs stay fixed, including 3.0x sales-to-capital, 17% operating tax, 3% terminal growth, 18% terminal ROIC, and the same cash, debt, claims and share count. It produces **$342.44 per share**, within $1.73 of the observed price [computed market_case_15pct_36pct_8pct]. About **77.5% of its enterprise value comes from discounted terminal value**, making the result sensitive to long-term assumptions. This case approximately sustains FY2026's 15.1% revenue growth for seven years while expanding EBIT margin from 33.0% to 36%. Near-term cost pressure and unresolved regulatory risks challenge that particular path. They do not establish necessary conditions for other combinations, which remain untested. A partial change in an input moves value continuously; it does not mechanically produce the house base value.

## Catalysts

**Memory cost trajectory:** Management guided Q1 FY2027 gross margin to 45–46% [S20]. If memory costs normalize faster than guided, gross margin recovery would be a positive catalyst. If they persist or worsen, the bear case becomes more probable.

**App Store regulatory outcomes:** The UK CMA SMS designation [S11] opens the door to conduct requirements. The DOJ lawsuit [S12] is at an earlier stage. Any settlement, court ruling, or regulatory order that materially alters App Store fee structures would be a negative catalyst for services revenue. This evidence more directly falsifies the bull case (which assumes regulatory outcomes are manageable) than the house base case, but it is a headwind to both.

**AI monetization evidence:** The FY2026 R&D increase of $11.2 billion year-over-year [S18 E3] reflects higher infrastructure costs including AI investments and headcount-related expenses per management characterization in the 10-K MD&A (specific sub-component breakdown not available in the supplied excerpts) [S19]; the AI-specific portion is not separately disclosed and the full increment is not a reliable AI-spend proxy. Evidence of AI-driven services revenue acceleration — or its absence — in subsequent quarters would be decision-changing.

**iPhone Duo cycle:** Q4 FY2026 iPhone revenue of $57.6 billion was up 17% year-over-year [S7], described as a September quarter record [S3]. Whether the iPhone Duo sustains that momentum through the December quarter and into FY2027 is a near-term observable.

**Share count and capital return:** The $100 billion repurchase authorization [S18 E7] is discretionary and non-obligatory. If shares are repurchased below intrinsic value, value per remaining share increases all else equal. At the current market price, repurchases are value-destructive relative to the house estimate.

## Risks and Disconfirming Evidence

**App Store economics:** The CMA designation and DOJ proceedings create uncertainty about future fees and platform conduct [S11, S12]. They are not equivalent to a realized change in reported revenue. Material fee reductions could pressure the services forecasts, but the available revenue breakdown does not permit a precise estimate of App Store exposure. The house expected impact is a judgment, not an observed market consensus.

**Component costs:** Management's Q1 FY2027 gross-margin guidance of 45–46% identifies near-term memory-cost pressure [S20]. Continued pressure would challenge the modeled operating margins; one quarter's guidance does not establish outcomes over seven years.

**AI economics:** FY2026 R&D increased by $11.2 billion, with infrastructure including AI and headcount cited as drivers [S19]. The AI-specific amount, duration and benefits remain uncertain. Defensive spending can protect retention, pricing and existing profits. Recurring costs exceeding incremental or protected profits would pressure margins; unknown benefits should not be treated as zero.

**Execution and geography:** The management transition, dependence on product-cycle execution, and Greater China's 17.2% revenue share introduce uncertainty that is not separately quantified in the model [S7, S18]. No proxy statement or detailed management-priority evidence has been reviewed.

**Evidence challenging the bear case:** Services revenue grew approximately 13% and Greater China revenue approximately 28% in FY2026, while the active installed base reached a record [S3, S7]. These observations raise the burden of proof for an abrupt slowdown. A record installed-base level is a stock measure, not its growth rate, and does not rule out deceleration.

**Evidence that would change the house view:** House monitoring triggers are four consecutive quarters of at least 15% revenue growth with EBIT margin trending toward 36%, gross-margin recovery despite the identified memory-cost pressure, or implemented regulatory remedies that preserve observable Services profitability. These are analyst-selected decision thresholds, not reported outcomes or sufficient conditions for a price target. Such evidence would challenge the base growth fade and margin compression and warrant replacing the corresponding forecast inputs. Evidence of lower required returns would separately warrant recalibrating WACC. None is established by the present source set. Fifteen percent growth alone produces $232.25 with the remaining original base inputs fixed; the 15%/36%/8% combination produces $342.44 [computed counterfactuals]. Neither isolated observations nor a single quarter automatically establish the persistence of either path.

## Evidence Gaps and Unresolved Questions

- **Services exposure:** Total services revenue is disclosed, but the available evidence does not isolate App Store fees from advertising, cloud, subscriptions and other services. The regulatory earnings impact cannot be precisely quantified [S7, S18].
- **AI investment:** AI-specific capital expenditure, operating costs and revenue benefits are not separately established. The full R&D increase is not a reliable measure of AI spending [S19].
- **Tax normalization:** The FY2024 rate included the disclosed one-time Ireland charge. The 17% forward operating tax rate remains a house assumption; the 20% and 24% stress cases show its valuation sensitivity [S19; computed_diagnostics].
- **Discount rate and terminal economics:** Current risk-free rate, beta, equity-risk premium and debt weights were not independently calibrated. The 9.5% WACC and terminal ROIC assumptions are explicit judgments. The model quantifies the terminal reinvestment discontinuity and alternatives; neither is an observed forecast outcome.
- **Cash and claims:** Classifying all reported cash and securities as nonoperating does not establish that all are economically surplus. Additional unidentified equity claims are assumed zero, not proved absent. Finance leases are deducted once outside financial net debt [S19; reported inputs].
- **Operating leases:** The reported liability is $12.912 billion as of September 26, 2026. GAAP EBIT already retains operating rent; capitalization would require consistent EBIT and reinvestment adjustments before changing the equity bridge [S19].

The $151.71 weighted estimate is conditional on the three selected house scenarios. They do not exhaust the range of possible outcomes. The computed counterfactual demonstrates how sustained growth, a higher operating margin and a lower discount rate can produce a different value; its economic plausibility and persistence require judgment. The source gaps and sensitivities should inform that judgment, rather than be treated as proof of a unique fair price.
